        <div class="bio-cards">
            <div class="bio-card"><span class="icon">🌱</span><h3>Early Years &amp; Training</h3><p>Born in Whakatāne in 1955, <span class="hl-cyan">Kilda Mori Northcott MNZM</span> trained in classical ballet at the <span class="hl-orange">Ruby Conway School of Dance</span> in Kawerau, then the <span class="hl-purple">New Zealand Dance Centre</span> under Russell Kerr and Basil Pattison. Her foundation was classical, but her curiosity led her to New York to study with <span class="hl-purple">Merce Cunningham</span> and <span class="hl-purple">José Limón</span>, whose revolutionary ideas shaped her artistic philosophy.</p></div>
            <div class="bio-card"><span class="icon">🔥</span><h3>Limbs Dance Company (1977–1983)</h3><p>In 1977 she became a founding member of <span class="hl-cyan">Limbs Dance Company</span>, born at Rongomaraeroa Marae. This era was defined by populist modernism—performing in nightclubs, prisons, and for royalty at the 1981 Royal Variety Performance. Limbs broke down the "fourth wall" and became the first contemporary dance company to achieve national popularity.</p></div>
            <div class="bio-card"><span class="icon">🎭</span><h3>The Douglas Wright Muse (1989–2003)</h3><p>Often cited as the "spine" of Douglas Wright's most iconic works. Wright called her his artistic muse, writing in <span class="hl-gold italic">Ghost Dance</span>: <em>"She danced in the same way the Mona Lisa smiles—calm, inscrutable, seeing nothing, seeing everything."</em> Together they created landmark works that toured Australia and Europe.</p></div>
            <div class="bio-card"><span class="icon">🧘</span><h3>Bipeds, Pedagogy &amp; Maturity</h3><p>Co-founding <span class="hl-cyan">Bipeds Productions</span>, Northcott became a champion for mature performers, challenging the notion that dance is only for the young. She received the MNZM for her services to dance, and continues to mentor generations. Her collaborations with <span class="hl-cyan">Alyx Duncan</span> have brought her movement to the screen in haunting cinematic narratives.</p></div>
